Hot shot trucking has exploded over the past decade. Owner-operators running 1-ton pickups with gooseneck trailers are filling freight lanes that traditional carriers can't serve economically. But with that flexibility comes a more complex insurance picture than most new operators expect.

## What Makes Hot Shot Insurance Different

Hot shot operators are commercial carriers. The moment you haul freight for compensation, you need commercial insurance — personal auto won't cover you, and your motor carrier won't be happy when they find out you're uninsured.

The complexity comes from the gaps:

- Your motor carrier authority policy covers you when you're dispatched with a load
- Bobtail insurance covers you when you're running without a trailer
- Non-trucking liability covers personal use of your commercial truck
- Trailer interchange covers non-owned trailers you haul under agreements
- Cargo covers the freight itself

Miss any one of these, and you have a gap that could cost you everything.

## The FMCSA Authority Filing

Your MC number is your license to haul for hire across state lines. Getting it is straightforward — the complexity is the insurance filings that must accompany it.

**MCS-90 endorsement**: This federal endorsement attaches to your auto liability policy and is required for all for-hire interstate carriers. It obligates your insurer to cover certain losses that your policy might otherwise exclude.

**BMC-91 filing**: Required for most for-hire carriers. It notifies FMCSA that you have the required liability coverage.

## Cargo Coverage: What to Ask For

FMCSA minimums for cargo are much lower than what freight brokers actually require. The federal floor is $10,000 for general freight — but most brokers won't give you a load unless you carry $100,000 or more.

For specialty loads — heavy equipment, oilfield supply, high-value machinery — brokers often require $250,000 or higher. Build your cargo limits around your highest-value typical load, not the minimum.

## Physical Damage: ACV vs. Stated Value

Your truck is your business. Insuring it properly matters.

Actual Cash Value (ACV) pays the depreciated market value at the time of loss. For a 5-year-old truck that cost $70,000 new, ACV might only be $35,000 — even if it's in perfect condition and worth more to you running.

Stated Value locks in an amount you choose at policy inception. You document the condition and value upfront, and that's what you get paid on a total loss.

For working hot shot trucks, we almost always recommend stated value.

## Building Your Complete Program

A proper hot shot program includes:
1. Motor carrier authority (FMCSA filings + auto liability)
2. Cargo insurance
3. Physical damage on your truck and trailer(s)
4. Bobtail insurance
5. Non-trucking liability
6. Trailer interchange (if you haul non-owned trailers)
7. Occupational accident (if you're an owner-operator without workers' comp)

Getting all of these in place before your first load protects you from day one.
