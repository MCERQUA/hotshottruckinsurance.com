If you've ever gotten a quote for hot shot insurance, someone probably asked whether you need bobtail or non-trucking liability. Many operators assume they're the same thing. They're not — and understanding the difference could prevent a six-figure claim denial.

## What Is Bobtail Insurance?

Bobtail insurance covers your liability when you're operating your truck without a trailer attached — regardless of whether you're on or off dispatch.

The name comes from "bobtailing" — a trucker's term for running without a trailer. The exposure is real: an empty 1-ton pickup at highway speed is still a significant liability risk if you cause an accident.

Common bobtail scenarios:
- Driving to the pickup location before attaching a trailer
- Returning home after dropping a load
- Running to a fuel stop between deliveries
- Repositioning your rig between loads

## What Is Non-Trucking Liability?

Non-trucking liability (NTL) covers your personal, non-business use of your commercial truck when you're not under dispatch for a motor carrier.

Common NTL scenarios:
- Driving to the grocery store in your work truck on the weekend
- Using your truck for personal errands between loads
- A family member driving your commercial truck for personal purposes
- Time off between dispatch periods

The key distinction: NTL is about dispatch status, not trailer status. Bobtail is about trailer status.

## Why You Might Need Both

Owner-operators leased to a motor carrier often need both policies. Here's why:

Your motor carrier's policy covers you when you're dispatched with a load. Period. Everything else falls into a gap.

If you're driving to the pickup location (no trailer, but heading to a load) — bobtail covers you.
If you're grocery shopping in your truck on a day off — non-trucking covers you.
If you're driving home after dropping a load without a trailer — bobtail covers you.
If your spouse borrows the truck for personal use — non-trucking covers that.

## If You Own Your Own Authority

If you carry your own MC number and have your own motor carrier auto liability policy, the coverage situation is different. Your policy should cover you any time you're operating your truck for business purposes — with or without a trailer, on or off a specific dispatch.

You may still need non-trucking liability for personal use, but bobtail coverage as a separate policy is less critical if your own motor carrier policy is comprehensive.

## The Bottom Line

When in doubt, ask your agent to walk through your specific operating scenario. Tell them:
- Whether you're leased to a carrier or have your own authority
- How much personal use your truck sees
- How many deadhead miles you typically run

That conversation will determine exactly which combination of coverage you need.
