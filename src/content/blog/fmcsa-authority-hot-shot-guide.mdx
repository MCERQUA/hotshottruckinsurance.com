Getting your federal motor carrier authority (MC number) is one of the most important steps in launching a hot shot operation. It's also one of the most misunderstood. Here's what you actually need — and how insurance fits into the process.

## Do You Need an MC Number?

If you're hauling freight for compensation across state lines, yes — you need an MC number. The threshold is simple: for-hire, interstate, commercial cargo.

Intrastate operations (staying within one state) may only require a USDOT number in some states, but requirements vary widely. Many states now require additional state authority filings even for intrastate carriers.

## What's the Difference Between USDOT and MC Number?

**USDOT number**: Identifies your motor carrier in FMCSA's systems. Required for commercial vehicles above certain weight thresholds operating in interstate commerce. This is a registration, not an authority.

**MC number (motor carrier authority)**: Your permission to haul freight for compensation. For-hire carriers need this. It's the license that says you can legally carry other people's freight and get paid for it.

Most hot shot operators need both.

## The Insurance You Need Before Going Active

FMCSA won't activate your authority until you have proper insurance filings on file. Here's what's required:

**Auto liability**: Minimum $750,000 for general freight, $1,000,000 for most hazmat, and more for specific hazmat categories. Most brokers require $1,000,000 regardless of commodity.

**MCS-90 endorsement**: Attached to your auto liability policy. Required for all for-hire interstate carriers. It obligates your insurer to cover certain federal liability obligations.

**BMC-91 or BMC-91X filing**: Your insurer files this with FMCSA to confirm your coverage is in place. Without this filing, your authority won't go active.

**Cargo insurance (optional for many)**: FMCSA doesn't require cargo insurance for general freight hot shots (only for household goods movers). But every freight broker will require it — so it's practically mandatory.

## The Timeline

After you apply for authority and your insurance filings are submitted:
- The 10-day "protest period" begins (allows existing carriers to object)
- If no protests, authority is granted after 10 days
- FMCSA systems typically update within 24–48 hours of insurance filing

Total timeline from application to first load: usually 3–4 weeks if everything goes smoothly.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Choosing minimum coverage to save money**: That $750,000 liability limit won't satisfy most freight brokers, who require $1 million. You'll have to upgrade anyway.

**Not getting cargo coverage**: FMCSA doesn't require it, but no broker will tender you a load without it. Build cargo into your program from day one.

**Forgetting bobtail coverage**: Your authority policy covers you under dispatch. Running empty to a pickup or returning home without a trailer is uninsured without bobtail.

**Not listing all drivers**: Every driver operating under your authority must be listed on your insurance. Missing drivers can cause claim denials.
