Hot shot truckers often haul trailers they don't own. A freight broker assigns you a flatbed sitting at a shipper's yard. A carrier gives you access to a step-deck for a specific lane. You use a rental gooseneck for a specialized load.

In every one of these scenarios, you're physically responsible for a piece of equipment worth $15,000 to $80,000 or more. If it gets damaged while you're hauling it, who pays?

Without trailer interchange coverage — you do.

## What Is Trailer Interchange Coverage?

Trailer interchange coverage pays for physical damage to trailers you don't own but are hauling under a written interchange agreement. It's essentially physical damage coverage for borrowed or assigned equipment.

What it covers:
- Collision damage to the interchange trailer
- Comprehensive losses (theft, fire, hail, vandalism)
- Damage during loading or unloading
- Equipment assigned through a freight broker agreement
- Trailers under carrier interchange agreements

What it doesn't cover:
- Trailers you own (those are on your physical damage policy)
- Cargo inside the trailer (that's cargo coverage)
- Liability for damage you cause to third parties (that's your auto liability)

## When Do You Need It?

You need trailer interchange coverage when all three of these are true:
1. You're hauling a trailer you don't own
2. There's a written agreement (even just a broker's load tender with terms) making you responsible for the equipment
3. The trailer owner doesn't have insurance that covers damage caused by operators hauling it

Most modern load board and broker agreements include language that transfers physical responsibility for the trailer to you while it's in your possession. That language is your signal that you need trailer interchange.

## Trailer Interchange vs. Non-Owned Trailer Coverage

These sound similar but work differently:

**Non-owned trailer coverage** is broader and can cover trailers you occasionally borrow without a specific written agreement. It's more flexible.

**Trailer interchange** specifically covers trailers under a written interchange agreement. Many commercial trucking policies include or specifically address interchange agreements.

Your agent will review your typical operating scenarios and recommend the right coverage type.

## How Much Coverage Do You Need?

Match your trailer interchange limits to the maximum value of equipment you're likely to haul:

- Basic aluminum flatbed: $15,000–$25,000
- Steel flatbed with ramps: $20,000–$35,000
- Gooseneck with living quarters: $40,000–$80,000
- Specialty enclosed trailer: $50,000+

It's better to slightly over-insure here than to be caught with a $60,000 trailer and a $30,000 limit.

## Adding It to Your Program

Trailer interchange is typically added as an endorsement to your commercial trucking policy. The premium is usually modest — a few hundred dollars per year in most cases — and it closes a gap that could otherwise result in a five-figure out-of-pocket loss.
